App / 07

Risk Assessment

Score likelihood per threat with the method of the project's preset, combine it with the impact into a risk level, prioritize with MoSCoW, and compare risk before and after controls.

01

Risk Scoring

  • Impact comes from the security goals the threat violates on its linked assets, including per-goal impact
  • The likelihood method follows the preset the regulation tag selects — each level named by the situation it stands for:
    • Standard — skill level, motive, opportunity, ease of exploit, weighted mean
    • ISO/SAE 21434 — attack potential per ISO/IEC 18045: elapsed time, expertise, knowledge, window of opportunity, equipment
    • ETSI TVRA — TS 102 165-1: time, expertise, knowledge, opportunity, equipment, intensity
    • EN 50742 A — attack potential AP = (EL × WoO) + AC: exposure level and attacker capability per risk, window of opportunity once for the project
  • Risk = Impact × Likelihood on a configurable 3-, 4- or 5-level matrix
  • Risk levels from Low to Critical, depending on the scale — thresholds configurable per project; levels and colours follow your own thresholds everywhere, and the matrix cells agree with the risk chips
  • EN 50742 A: each risk gets its SRSL (Table B.6) from the attack-potential band and the severity on the safety function — reversible, non-reversible, or fatal as a TARAflow extension; the reference tables are in the risk dialog
  • ISO/SAE 21434: the risk value 1–5 (clause 15.8) from the matrix of Annex H.8 replaces I × L — impact on the 4-level scale Negligible / Moderate / Major / Severe × attack feasibility, shown with a word (Minimal / Low / Medium / High / Critical); the heat map is the 4 × 4 matrix of H.8
02

Impact Aggregation

  • Harm floor (standard, EN 50742): safety, physical damage and environment are never diluted — the impact is the highest of them or the weighted mean of the other factors
  • Maximum (ISO/SAE 21434, ETSI TVRA): the worst impact category, unweighted
  • Impact weights default to the asset criterion weights and can be overridden per factor
  • Switching the aggregation shows every risk that changes before it is applied; the risk dialog and the report explain how each impact was formed
03

Prioritization

  • MoSCoW: Must fix / Should fix / Could fix / Won't fix
  • ISO/SAE 21434: the attack paths of one tree form a threat scenario — its value and residual are the highest of its paths; treatment options Avoid / Reduce / Share / Retain (15.9); a review list names Retain at a value of 4 or more and Reduce at value 1 — nothing changes automatically
  • Before/after toggle: compare risk score before and after proposed mitigations
  • Treatment options Eliminate / Reduce / Accept / Transfer / Share
  • Won't risks are listed in their own table with their justification; a missing justification is flagged
  • Risk register exportable to documentation and ticket systems
04

Risk Dialog

  • Feasibility | Impact | Risk, each with where it comes from — attack-tree path and attack potential, damage scenario
  • The residual is rated with the five attack-potential factors, listed least likely first
05

Cybersecurity Goals & Claims

  • ISO/SAE 21434 projects: a tab Goal / Claim in the risk dialog
  • A cybersecurity goal (CSG-nn, optional CAL) for every risk you reduce, proposed from the asset's security goal
  • A claim (CLM-nn) for every risk you retain (based on assumptions) or share (with the responsible party)
  • The Goals & Claims dialog shows everything per threat scenario, proposes missing goals and lists the findings
06

Leveled DFDs

  • Risks are grouped per decomposed multiprocess, like the threats
  • One risk matrix per DFD group instead of a single roll-up
  • Filter by page and analysis state
